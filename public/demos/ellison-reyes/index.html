      Questions families ask

      Straight answers.

      Still unsure? Call. We'll walk you through it with no obligation.

      How much does a bail bond cost in California?

      The premium is set by the California Department of Insurance at 10% of the bail amount, or 8% for military, union members, AARP members and clients with a private attorney. It's a fee for the bond and isn't refunded.

      Do I need collateral?

      Most bonds under $50,000 only need a cosigner with steady income. Larger bonds may need a vehicle title or property, which is returned when the bond is exonerated.

      How long does release take?

      Usually two to ten hours after we post, depending on the facility, the time of night and any holds.

      What if they miss a court date?

      Call us immediately. California gives 180 days (plus 5 for mailing) to resolve a forfeiture, and most missed dates can be fixed quickly when we move fast.

      Can I do everything from my phone?

      Yes. Apply, sign, pay and get updates by text. You can still come to either office any hour.

      What is a bail bond?

      When bail is set, the full amount must be guaranteed before release. A bail bond is our promise to the court to pay it if the defendant doesn't appear. You pay a premium instead of the full bail.

      Who can be a cosigner?

      Usually a family member or close friend with steady income who lives locally. The cosigner (indemnitor) agrees the defendant will attend court and covers the bail if they don't and can't be returned.

      What do I need to start?

      Their full name, date of birth and the jail or arrest city. Your ID and a way to pay the down payment. A booking number helps but isn't required.

      What is a hold?

      An immigration, probation or out-of-county warrant can keep someone in custody even after bail is posted. We check for holds before you pay.

      What happens at the end of the case?

      When the case is resolved by dismissal or sentencing, the court exonerates the bond. We close the file and return any collateral.

      What if they miss court?

      Call us right away. Under Penal Code §1305 there's a 180-day window to return the defendant before the forfeiture becomes final. Most missed dates are fixed by rescheduling quickly.
