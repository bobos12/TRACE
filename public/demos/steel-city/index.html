How bail works · Pennsylvania

How bail works
in Allegheny County

Pennsylvania has five kinds of bail. Only one of them, monetary bail, ever needs a bondsman. Here's the whole process, in order.

§Details

The fine print,
in plain English.

Preliminary arraignment

After intake at the Allegheny County Jail, the defendant sees a magistrate, often by video from the jail. The magistrate reads the charges and sets bail. For bail questions, the arraignment court at Pittsburgh Municipal Court, 660 First Ave, can be reached at (412) 350-3240.

The 10% deposit

Under Pennsylvania Rule 528, the bail authority may let the defendant post a cash deposit of up to 10% of monetary bail directly with the court. The defendant then signs as their own surety and is liable for the full amount if they skip court. If that option was granted, call us anyway, and we'll tell you if you don't need us.

A surety bond

When monetary bail is set and a 10% deposit isn't allowed, or you can't cover it, a licensed bondsman posts the full bail. You pay a premium (ours is 10%, $100 minimum) and a cosigner signs an indemnity agreement. Larger bonds may need collateral.

Holds & detainers

A state parole or county probation detainer, another county's warrant, or an immigration detainer can keep someone in custody after bail is posted. We check for holds before you pay.

Release

After the bond is posted, ACJ processes the release. It typically takes a few hours, sometimes longer at shift change. We'll call you when it starts moving.

Exoneration

When the case is over, the bond is exonerated: the obligation ends and any collateral is returned. Miss court and the bond can be forfeited, and the cosigner may owe the full bail.
